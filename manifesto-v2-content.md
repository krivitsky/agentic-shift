## Hero

**Agentic Shift**

---

## Opening

Something is being sold to you alongside the models. Not only the tools — a set of working habits, arriving as advice. Generate more. Review less. Let the craft go.

We don't think this is a conspiracy. We think it's a very good strategy. Every company chases product-market fit; when your product is a frontier model, one of the available routes is to change the market rather than the product — and habits are part of the market. An engineer who still reads code, judges it, and holds the design is a harder fit than one who has stopped.

So the advice is real advice, and it is also doing strategic work. Both are true at once.

---

## 1. What we optimize for

Everyone is optimizing for something.

The frontier labs optimize for adoption and throughput. That is legitimate, and it is their business — not yours.

We optimize for two things: **the value delivered to customers**, and **the health of the organization that delivers it** — its capacity to keep learning, keep judging, and keep changing direction.

These are not the same target. Where they diverge, we follow ours.

---

## 2. Who sets the direction

Direction is not something you receive.

An organization that doesn't know what it is trying to become will adopt whichever practice is being promoted most loudly, and will call that a strategy.

Which capabilities you grow, and which you are content to rent, is a design decision. It is being made inside your organization right now — by default, by whoever adopts fastest. We think it belongs to leadership, made deliberately, before the tools arrive rather than after.

A consultancy sells you a contract. It ends, and you still have your organization. This has a different shape: if the reading and the judging quietly stop, there is no contract to end. There is only what your people can still do without the tools.

---

## 3. What we refuse

We do not treat the loss of judgment as progress.

We do not accept dependence as a design outcome.

We do not confuse volume of output with capability.

---

## 4. What we build

Professional agentic product engineering is what that decision looks like when it is carried through — a set of noticeable org-design shifts in how product engineering work gets done.

Each shift has a half that nobody sells you. That half is the work.

### 1. features → systems
**The move.** Stop shipping one-off features.
**The half nobody sells you.** The self-improving loop that turns intent into working solutions — and someone accountable for the loop itself, not just for its output.
**Skip it and:** you get more output and the same product.

### 2. gatekeeping → guardrails
**The move.** Stop inspecting for quality at the end.
**The half nobody sells you.** Quality engineered into the system — the tests, the specifications, the coverage, the harness that makes output trustworthy before anyone looks at it.
**Skip it and:** you have simply removed review. Teams who stop inspecting without building the harness ship faster for two quarters, then cannot explain their own incidents.

### 3. specialists → craftspeople
**The move.** Stop assembly-line specialization.
**The half nobody sells you.** Craftspeople who pull in specialist depth on demand and grow from it — the human is the one acting, not the one being filled in for.
**Skip it and:** you replace narrow specialists with generalists who no longer know what they don't know.

### 4. proxies → users
**The move.** Stop the telephone game through manager-proxies.
**The half nobody sells you.** The org structure that permits zero distance to customers — a mandate, not an encouragement.
**Skip it and:** you deliver the wrong thing faster.

### 5. output → outcomes
**The move.** Stop measuring people by output.
**The half nobody sells you.** Teams that sense drift and correct course themselves, which requires the mandate to change direction.
**Skip it and:** you get outcome language over output management, and nothing changes but the vocabulary.

---

**The first two shifts are technical** — they draw on the [Agentic Engineer's Field Guide](https://agentic-engineering.guide/).

**The other three are structural** — they create the conditions that put the technology to work, drawing on the adaptive org topology of [10X ORG](https://10xorg.com/).

**They form one system.** They enable and reinforce one another, and cannot be managed in isolation. The labs ship the technical half. The structural half has no owner unless you take it.

---

## Closing

While coding is being automated and routinized, the craft of product engineering is undergoing a major reinvention — one made possible only by distinctly human traits: **the drive for perfection**, **unlimited creativity**, and **rich co-creation**.

The task of every organization and every leader is to design environments that **elevate human intelligence** to these new heights.
